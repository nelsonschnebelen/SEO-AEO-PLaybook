02 · Not a prediction

This already happened. Here are the dates.

Everything below shipped. That is why the 2027 part is worth half an hour of your week rather than a line in next year’s budget.

  1. Aug 2026Live

    ChatGPT books your table

    Reservations and waitlists inside the conversation, through OpenTable (global), Resy (US) and Yelp (US and Canada). The Reserve button only appears when a supported provider is connected to your restaurant.

  2. Aug 2026Live

    Google Maps takes food orders

    A diner can ask for a specific dish along their route and add it to a cart through a partner ordering service. The dish name is now a search term.

  3. Apr 2026Live

    Google AI Mode books, and sometimes names one place

    Restaurant booking landed in several countries, and for some dining questions AI Mode returns a single AI-written recommendation rather than a list. One slot, not ten.

  4. Early 2026Rolling out

    Gemini turns your reviews into your description

    Google began generating local results that group review text into themes — what people order, what they come for. Whatever your reviewers keep repeating becomes the sentence an assistant says about you.

  5. Mar 2026Default on

    Shopify made 5.6M storefronts agent-readable overnight

    Agentic storefronts switched on by default in March, with llms.txt pushed to every store weeks later. Nobody opted in. Platforms will keep doing this — good news if yours does, a gap if yours doesn’t.

  6. Jun 2026Beta

    Siri rebuilt on a Gemini model

    Shown in developer beta. Any app that exposes App Intents can be driven by voice — so the booking and ordering apps you already use become the hands-free path to your door.

  7. Jul 2026Unreleased

    “Ask Maps to order food”

    Found in a Google Maps build, not switched on. No partners and no date announced — so treat it as direction, not a deadline.

How to read this page

Anything marked Live has shipped and you can test it today. Beta and Unreleased are direction, from reporting and app teardowns rather than announcements. Platforms change fast — check anything platform-specific against the official docs before you spend money on it.

05 · Features & plugins

Six jobs. That’s the whole stack.

An agent-ready restaurant site does six things. Here is each one, what it costs you in time, and what does the job on your platform.

1

Let the AI crawlers in

Blocker 10 min Web person

If robots.txt blocks OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot or ClaudeBot, you are not ranked low in those assistants — you are missing from them. A security plugin or CDN rule does this by default more often than anyone expects, and nobody notices because Google traffic looks fine.

Where: yourdomain.com/robots.txt, and your CDN or firewall rules separately — those can challenge a crawler no matter what robots.txt says.

2

Restaurant schema

20 min Plugin or paste

Your page says “open 5pm till late, Tues–Sun”. Schema says opens 17:00 for each day, plus price range, address, menu URL and acceptsReservations. Nothing left to interpret — which is exactly what an agent needs before it acts.

Where: your SEO plugin, or one pasted JSON-LD block. Generate yours below and validate it before publishing.

3

A menu made of text

1–2 hrs Your website

The test: open your menu and try to select a dish name with your cursor. Can’t? No assistant can read it. Google Maps now lets diners search for a specific dish along their route — a PDF menu means the dish you’re known for is invisible.

Where: a real menu page — headings per section, dish names as text, a line of description, prices, dietary tags. AI shortcut: photograph the printed menu, ask ChatGPT for clean HTML, then check every price yourself.

4

Answer pages

Half a day Your website

Assistants lift passages, not pages. Make the question the heading, phrased how a diner would ask it, and answer completely in the first 40–60 words. 15–25 real diner questions is the most quotable thing a restaurant can publish.

Where: ordinary pages, plus FAQPage schema. State what AI will never guess: price range, reservation policy, biggest party, private dining, parking, transit, accessibility, kids, dress code, last seating.

5

A connected booking or ordering provider

2027 critical Your provider

This is the one that turns a mention into a Reserve button. ChatGPT books through OpenTable, Resy and Yelp; Maps orders through partner ordering services. If your tables live in a notebook or a phone line, the assistant describes you and books someone else.

Where: OpenTable, Resy, Yelp, SevenRooms for tables; Toast, Olo, Square for orders. Ask your provider one question: are we live in ChatGPT and Maps, and what do you need from us?

6

A review flow that names dishes

Ongoing In the shift

Google now groups review text into themes — what people order, what they come for. A review that names the short rib is worth more than three that say “great!”. Whatever repeats becomes your description, good or bad.

Where: QR on the check, a line in the receipt email, a POS prompt. Five a shift is 150 a month. Never: gate the ask to happy diners, pay for reviews, or solicit on Yelp at all.

What does the job on your platform

And five things to skip

Where restaurant AI budgets go to die.

× Paying for llms.txt Independent log studies found AI crawlers almost never request it — 0.1% of AI bot visits in one 90-day test, and 88% of sites in another never got a single request. Google says no Search system uses it; OpenAI’s own docs still point to robots.txt. Publish it if your platform does it for free. Don’t buy it.
× “AEO tools” that promise AI rankings Nobody controls who gets named. Anyone guaranteeing you a spot is selling you something they cannot deliver. Do the six jobs first, for free.
× Marking up your own star rating Self-serving review schema is a policy violation and a common cause of penalties. Let the review platforms carry the stars.
× AI-written allergen or dietary info Never publish it without your kitchen checking it line by line. This is the one place a confident wrong answer hurts a person.
× Bought links and directory blasts Also keyword-stuffing your business name, which breaks Google’s rules outright, and blogging for its own sake. None of it moves an assistant.

The twelve chapters

The reference layer, ordered by payoff. Open the ones you need.

01Let the assistants inBlocked crawler = invisible. Check this before anything else.▸

Open yourdomain.com/robots.txt. If it blocks OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot or ClaudeBot, you are not ranked low in those assistants — you are absent from them.

A security plugin or CDN rule does this by default more often than anyone expects, and nobody notices because search traffic looks fine.

Also check your CDN or firewall separately. It can challenge those crawlers no matter what robots.txt says.

02Your Google listingThe biggest single source of the facts AI repeats about you.▸

When an assistant says “they take reservations and there’s parking behind the building”, that usually came from here.

  • Category — “Neapolitan pizza restaurant” beats “Restaurant”.
  • Attributes nobody fills in: dietary, accessibility, parking, payments. Each one is a question an assistant can answer about you.
  • Price range — a top filter, and AI won’t guess it.
  • Your website field — make sure it points at you, not a delivery app taking a cut.
03The menu problemA PDF menu is invisible. This one hour pays for itself.▸

The test: open your menu and try to select a dish name. Can’t? Then no assistant can read it — and someone asking for the exact dish you’re known for will never hear your name.

Rebuild it as a real page: headings per section, dish names as text, a line of description, prices, dietary tags.

AI shortcut: photograph your printed menu, hand it to ChatGPT, ask for clean HTML. Then check every price yourself.

04Say what AI will never guessUnstated means “I’m not sure”, and they move on.▸

Assistants do not infer. If your site never says you take reservations, the answer to “can I book there?” is a shrug.

Put these in plain text somewhere: price range, reservation policy and biggest party, private dining capacity, parking, transit, accessibility, kids, dress code, dietary accommodation, last seating.

05Write to be quotedQuestion as the heading. Answer in the first 40 words.▸

Assistants lift passages, not pages. The single most effective structural change you can make:

  • Make the question the heading — phrased how a diner would ask it
  • Answer completely in the first 40–60 words
  • Make each answer stand alone without the rest of the page
  • Detail and personality go after the answer

A page of 15–25 real diner questions is the most quotable thing a restaurant can publish.

06Reviews are what AI paraphrasesAsk if you're any good and it summarises strangers.▸

Whatever theme keeps repeating in your reviews is roughly what an assistant will say about you. If “slow on weekends” shows up eleven times, that sentence is now part of your description.

Build the ask into the shift — QR on the check, line in the receipt email. Five a shift is 150 a month.

Three ways to get burned: gating (only asking happy diners), paying, or soliciting on Yelp at all.

07Make your sources agreeConflicting facts make AI hedge — or skip you.▸

Your site says you close at 10, Google says 11, Yelp says 9:30. The assistant has three facts and no way to choose, so it says “hours vary, check ahead” — or picks somewhere unambiguous.

Pick your website as the source of truth and reconcile everything to it. Claim Apple Business Connect and Bing Places while you’re there — Bing feeds a share of Copilot.

08Spell it out in codeSchema: the one format that can't be misread.▸

Your page says “open 5pm till late, Tues–Sun”. Schema says opensAt 17:00 for each day, and there is nothing left to interpret.

Use the generator below, validate it, paste it in. Five minutes.

Never mark up your own star rating. Self-serving review markup is against Google’s rules and a common cause of penalties.

09Get onto the pages AI citesFaster than becoming a trusted source yourself.▸

Ask Perplexity for the best restaurants in your city and look at what it cites. That citation list is your outreach list.

Claim every aggregator that appears. Pitch the local publications. Enter the reader polls. Being on a page an assistant already trusts beats trying to earn that trust from scratch.

10Using AI without getting burnedBrilliant at writing. Confidently wrong about your facts.▸

Let it: turn a photographed menu into HTML, write dish descriptions, generate schema, draft review replies, summarise 100 reviews into themes.

Never let it: state your hours or prices unchecked, invent dishes, claim awards, or post to your Google profile unsupervised.

The allergen rule: never publish AI-written dietary or allergen info without your kitchen checking it line by line.

11Measuring itNo dashboard exists. You measure by asking.▸

Write 15 realistic diner questions. Ask them in ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Copilot on the same day each month. Log four things: were you named, in what position, were the facts right, and what did it cite.

That is the whole methodology, and it works. Chase every wrong fact back to the source that produced it.
