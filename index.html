05 · The playbook

Twelve chapters. Open the ones you need.

Ordered by payoff. If you only have one day, do chapter 1 and stop.

01The first afternoonFive things. Do these and you beat most of your block.▸
  • Claim and verify your Google Business Profile
  • Fix your hours — including every holiday for the next year
  • Set the most specific category that fits (not just “Restaurant”)
  • Get your menu out of the PDF
  • Start asking every guest for a review, tonight

Before you pay anyone: if they haven’t asked to see your Google profile first, they’re not doing local SEO.

02Google Business ProfileFree. An afternoon. Bigger than your website.▸

Google ranks you on relevance, distance and prominence. You can’t move the building — so win on relevance, which is fast.

  • Category is the biggest lever. “Neapolitan pizza restaurant” beats “Restaurant”.
  • Attributes nobody fills in: dietary, accessibility, parking, payments. Each is a filter you can win.
  • Photos weekly, not 40 at once.
  • Check your order buttons. Delivery apps attach their own links and take a cut of orders that were already yours.
03The menu problemIf it’s a PDF, it’s invisible. This one hour pays for itself.▸

The test: open your menu and try to select a dish name. Can’t? Then neither Google nor any AI can read it — and someone searching for the exact dish you’re known for will never find you.

Rebuild it as a real page: headings per section, dish names as text, a line of description, prices, dietary tags. Keep the PDF as a download if you like.

AI shortcut: photograph your printed menu, hand it to ChatGPT, ask for clean HTML. Fifteen minutes instead of an afternoon — then check every price yourself.

04A website that does its jobFast on a phone. Menu, hours, directions, book. That’s it.▸
  • Under 3 seconds on cellular
  • Address, phone and hours as text on every page — not an image
  • Tap-to-call number
  • Order and Reserve above the fold
  • A unique title tag per page with your city in it

What you don’t need: a blog nobody reads, an image slider, or a background video that eats 8MB before the menu loads.

05Content that wins locallyOwn your neighbourhood, not your city.▸

“Restaurants in Chicago” is unwinnable. “Where to eat near the Music Box Theatre” is yours for the taking — and it’s what people actually ask.

Build one Visit page: address, map, parking (what it costs, where to go if the lot’s full), transit, landmarks, accessibility, what to expect.

06Reviews are your sales copy nowWritten by strangers. Quoted by AI.▸

In order: velocity (steady beats spiky), volume vs whoever outranks you, rating (4.3–4.8 is the sweet spot), response rate, then content.

Build the ask into the shift — QR on the check, line in the receipt email, link in the booking text. Five a shift is 150 a month.

Three ways to get burned: gating (only asking happy diners), paying, or soliciting on Yelp at all. Each can wipe your review set.

07Schema, without a developerDescribe your restaurant. Let AI write the code.▸

Schema states your hours and address in a format machines can’t misread. It’s the single most technical thing here and it takes five minutes.

Use the generator below, paste the result into Google’s Rich Results Test, and if it complains, paste the error back into the AI.

Never mark up your own star rating. Self-serving review markup is against Google’s rules and a common cause of penalties.

08How AI actually picks a restaurantCan it find you, read you, trust you, and quote you?▸

The biggest fix is structure: make the question your heading, then answer it completely in the first 40–60 words. Assistants lift passages, not pages.

The second is stating the obvious. AI won’t guess. If your site never says you take reservations, the answer becomes “I’m not sure” and your diner moves on.

Then check you’re not blocking the crawlers — a security plugin quietly blocking OAI-SearchBot or PerplexityBot removes you from those answers entirely.

09Using AI without getting burnedBrilliant at writing. Confidently wrong about your facts.▸

Let it: turn a photographed menu into HTML, write dish descriptions, generate schema, draft review replies, summarise 100 reviews into themes.

Never let it: state your hours or prices unchecked, invent dishes, claim awards, or post to your Google profile unsupervised.

The allergen rule: never publish AI-written dietary or allergen info without your kitchen checking it line by line. That’s the one place speed isn’t worth it.

10More than one locationOne profile and one page per location. Always.▸

Never one page listing every address — it ranks for none of them. Make each location page genuinely different: its own photos, parking, landmarks, and the actual manager’s name.

After a move, hunt down the old listing. Stale addresses send people to a locked door for years.

11What to ignoreWhere restaurant marketing money goes to die.▸
  • Bought backlinks and “1,000 directory submissions”
  • Keyword-stuffing your business name (it’s a policy violation)
  • Blogging for its own sake
  • Chasing city-wide terms you can’t win
  • Anyone guaranteeing rankings
12What to measureRankings are vanity. Count the things that become covers.▸

Direction requests, calls, new reviews per month, and online orders. Check them monthly, not daily.

Be patient in the right places: Google profile changes show in days; website and schema take 4–12 weeks; reviews and authority compound over months. Judge it on a quarter.

Fill in the form and press Generate. Anything you have not supplied appears as a clear FILL_IN placeholder rather than being invented.

Validate before you publish

Paste the generated code into Google's Rich Results Test at search.google.com/test/rich-results before adding it to your site. If it reports errors, paste both the code and the errors into an AI assistant and ask for a fix — there is a prompt for exactly that in the library.
