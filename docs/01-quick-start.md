# Quick start — the first afternoon

> **Start by checking what you already have.** Open `index.html`, go to *Check my site*, and paste
> in your homepage source. It takes two minutes and tells you which of the items below you actually
> need — rather than guessing.

If you read nothing else in this playbook, do these five things. For a typical independent
restaurant they account for the large majority of the available upside, and none of them requires
a developer, an agency or a budget.

---

## 1. Claim and verify your Google Business Profile

**Time:** 30 minutes, plus verification wait · **Cost:** free

Search your restaurant name on Google. If you see "Own this business?" or "Claim this business",
it is not yours yet. Go to [google.com/business](https://www.google.com/business/), find your
listing, and claim it.

Two things people get wrong here:

- **Do not create a new listing if one already exists.** Claim the existing one. Creating a
  duplicate splits your reviews and rankings between two profiles that then compete with each other.
- **Check for old addresses too.** If you have moved, there may be a stale listing still pointing
  people at a locked door.

Everything else in local search depends on this one being done.

---

## 2. Fix your hours — including the next twelve months of holidays

**Time:** 30 minutes · **Cost:** free

Wrong hours are the single most expensive small error in this industry. Someone drives across town,
finds you closed, and leaves a one-star review that outlives the mistake by years. Google also
demotes profiles it detects as unreliable.

Set your regular hours, then sit down once and add **special hours** for every holiday and planned
closure for the next twelve months. Add *more hours* entries where the kitchen closes before the
bar, or where brunch, happy hour and delivery run on different windows.

---

## 3. Set the most specific accurate primary category

**Time:** 30 seconds · **Cost:** free

The instinct is to pick something broad so you show up for more searches. That is backwards.

- "Restaurant" puts you in a pool with every restaurant in the city.
- "Neapolitan pizza restaurant" puts you in a pool of maybe four — and Google still shows you for
  broader pizza searches, because it understands the relationship.

Pick the single most specific category that genuinely describes your main business, then add two to
five secondaries for real secondary services (Caterer, Bar, Breakfast restaurant, Takeout
restaurant). Do not add categories for things you do not do.

---

## 4. Get your menu out of the PDF

**Time:** 1–2 hours (about 15 minutes with AI) · **Cost:** free

**The test:** open your menu on your website and try to select a dish name with your cursor, or by
long-pressing on a phone. If you cannot select it as text, no search engine and no AI assistant can
read it either.

A menu trapped in a PDF, a JPEG or an image of a chalkboard means every dish name, price, allergen
note and description on it is invisible. Someone searching for the exact dish you are known for
will not find you, because as far as the machines are concerned you do not serve it.

Rebuild it as a normal web page: headings for each section, dish names as text, a sentence of
description, and prices. Keep the PDF as a download if your regulars like it — but the HTML page is
the real menu, and it is the one your Google profile should link to.

**The AI shortcut:** photograph each page of your printed menu, hand the images to an AI assistant,
and ask for clean HTML with sections, dish names, descriptions, prices and dietary tags. Then check
every price yourself — this is exactly the kind of detail AI gets subtly wrong, and a wrong price on
your website is an argument at the table. The prompt is in
[the library](04-ai-prompt-library.md#turn-a-photographed-menu-into-a-real-web-page).

---

## 5. Start asking every guest for a review — tonight

**Time:** ongoing · **Cost:** free

Not a campaign. A habit. Put a QR code on the check presenter, a line in the receipt email, a link
in the booking confirmation, and brief the team to mention it naturally at a good moment. Five asks
a shift is roughly 150 a month, which transforms a profile within a quarter.

Three ways to get yourself penalized, so do not do them:

- **Do not gate.** Asking only the happy customers — or screening people through a feedback form
  and sending only the pleased ones to Google — violates platform policy and gets review sets wiped.
- **Do not pay.** Not in cash, discounts, free desserts or prize-draw entries.
- **Do not solicit on Yelp at all.** Yelp forbids asking. There you improve by responding and by
  fixing what people complain about.

## Before you buy anything

If someone is selling you "SEO services" for a restaurant and has not first asked to see your
Google Business Profile, they are not doing local SEO. Do the list above yourself first. Then, if
you hire help, you will actually be able to tell whether they are adding anything.

Be especially wary of anyone guaranteeing rankings. Nobody controls Google's results, and the people
who claim to are usually using techniques that eventually cost you.
